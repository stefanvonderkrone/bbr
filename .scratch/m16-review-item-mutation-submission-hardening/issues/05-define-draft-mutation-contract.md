# Define the Draft mutation contract

Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Blocked by: 02, 04, 14

## Question

How should body edits, root re-anchoring, failed-state reset, cascading deletion, active or recovered SubmissionRun immutability, persistence-before-publication, ScopeProjection rebuilding, and local-versus-remote Review behavior compose into one exhaustive Draft mutation contract satisfying `.scratch/review-item-mutation/issues/01-edit-reanchor-delete-drafts.md`?

## Answer

Draft mutation is a local, typed-identity operation over a repository-qualified PendingReview. A `draft` or `failed` Draft is mutable until it participates in an active or recovered SubmissionRun. The participating graph is immutable for the run's lifetime, not merely while each item individually carries `submitting`: changing any member could invalidate the run's captured topology, parent remapping, Duplicate guard, or persisted outcomes. `submitting`, transient `posted`, and `outcome_unknown` records are never locally mutable. A posted item is subsequently an author-owned Bitbucket Comment governed by [Define the author-owned published Comment mutation contract](06-define-published-comment-mutation-contract.md), not a mutable Draft.

ActionAvailability keeps edit, re-anchor, and delete discoverable on an ineligible Draft and reports the precise reason: the active SubmissionRun owns it, its publication outcome must be resolved, it is already represented by Bitbucket, or its shape does not support that Action. A recovered ambiguous item retains the amber/orange `outcome unknown - resolve before editing` treatment; confirmed failures remain red, and labels carry the meaning independently of color. Read-only Duplicate-guard reconciliation may change ambiguous `submitting` to `posted(CommentId)`. If automatic reconciliation cannot establish ownership, linking an author-owned Bitbucket Comment records `posted(CommentId)`, confirming unpublished returns the Draft to `draft`, and deciding later leaves it immutable.

Recovery also exposes **Abandon recovery** when the run cannot be completed. It atomically issues no further POSTs, converts the in-flight ambiguous `submitting` item to `outcome_unknown`, closes the SubmissionRun as terminal-partial, and releases its lock. Ordinary `draft` and `failed` items are then mutable again, while the ambiguous item remains immutable until linked or confirmed unpublished. Abandonment never asserts that Bitbucket did not publish the Comment and never discards Draft evidence.

Editing uses the same Composer interaction and validation as creating that Comment, Reply, or Suggestion, with the previous editable content prefilled. It preserves TempId, Draft kind, parent relationship, CommentScope, Anchor, and AnchorSnapshot. A Suggestion Composer exposes only the replacement code; bbr retains the `suggestion` kind and regenerates the fenced Markdown stored and sent through Bitbucket's `content.raw`. Blank or otherwise invalid content is refused by the same rules as creation. A real body change on a `failed` Draft resets it to `draft`; accepting byte-identical content is a no-op that retains the failure and its reason. Transient failures are retried through Submission rather than cleared by an unchanged edit.

Re-anchor applies only to an existing inline root Draft. It never converts Review-level or File-level CommentScope, and Replies remain ineligible because they inherit the root's scope. The two-stage interaction preserves TempId, kind, body, and descendants while replacing the Anchor from the current source cursor or Selection. Existing refusal rules remain structural: one File and one side, matched and ascending range coordinates, no hunk gap, at most 30 inclusive lines, and no old-side Suggestion. Re-anchoring a Suggestion preserves its replacement code. A LocalReview also replaces the root's immutable AnchorSnapshot from the newly selected source; a Remote review stores the side-appropriate commit identity but no local snapshot. Choosing the identical Anchor is a no-op. A real Anchor change resets `failed` to `draft`, replaces the root's ScopeProjection with `current`, and makes every Reply reuse that entry.

Delete requires a typed-target confirmation and reports the complete Draft reply-descendant count. One atomic store operation deletes the target and every descendant reached through `Parent.draft`; it does not infer a remote cascade. If any member of the proposed subtree is immutable, the whole deletion is refused so ambiguous or posted evidence cannot disappear. The in-memory graph, root ScopeProjection entry, descendant render rows, and counts change only after the complete cascade is durable.

Every accepted mutation follows stage → persist → publish. Presentation privately stages the candidate PendingReview, ScopeProjection, Buffer, and identity-based navigation before calling a transaction-shaped PendingReviewStore mutation. The store rechecks Draft identity, eligible state, SubmissionRun ownership, graph closure, and expected ReviewIdentity atomically; edit or re-anchor replaces one Draft, while delete removes the complete subtree in one transaction. After persistence succeeds, publishing the staged aggregate is infallible. Allocation, validation, Buffer construction, concurrent-state mismatch, or persistence failure preserves the previous ReviewCard, graph, ScopeProjection, Buffer, and navigation.

Failure also preserves the active interaction and the reviewer's proposed input: the edit Composer retains its changed body, re-anchor retains its candidate Anchor, and delete retains its confirmation Overlay, each with a specific retryable error. On success, edit restores the ReviewCard by TempId, re-anchor follows that TempId to its new projected ReviewCard, and delete selects the next surviving semantic row with previous/nearest-source fallback. An unchanged edit or identical re-anchor exits as a clean no-op without persistence or failure reset.

Remote and LocalReview Drafts share this contract. LocalReview has no SubmissionRun and never POSTs, but uses the same durable mutations, current ScopeProjection replacement, cascade rules, and persistence ordering; its re-anchor additionally captures the new AnchorSnapshot. Remote Drafts gain the SubmissionRun eligibility checks and eventually cross into Bitbucket authority after posting and Reconciliation. Session replacement always reloads the persisted graph and rebuilds ScopeProjection, so an accepted mutation survives refresh, review switching, restart, and stale in-memory Frames without introducing mutable authored scope into Presentation.

Deterministic coverage must exercise Comment, Reply, and Suggestion edits; identical no-ops; new- and old-side root re-anchor validation and the 30-line boundary; Suggestion code/fence handling; failed-state reset; atomic descendant deletion and immutable-subtree refusal; live and recovered run locks; link/unpublished/decide-later ambiguity outcomes; Abandon recovery; allocation, Buffer, and store rollback with interaction retention; identity-based cursor restoration; LocalReview snapshot replacement; RemoteReview Reconciliation boundaries; and Session replacement/reload from persisted mutations.
